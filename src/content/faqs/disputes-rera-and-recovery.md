  - question: 'What are the strict statutory timelines for a Section 138 cheque bounce case?'
    answer: 'First, the cheque must be presented within 3 months of its date (validity period). Second, upon dishonour, the payee must issue a written statutory demand notice within 30 days of receiving the bank return memo. Third, the drawer has 15 days from receipt of notice to make payment. Fourth, if unpaid, the criminal complaint must be filed before the jurisdictional Magistrate within 30 days after the 15-day cure period expires.'
  - question: 'How are RERA complaints filed and adjudicated for real estate projects in India?'
    answer: 'Complaints regarding registered real estate projects are filed online through the respective State RERA portal under Section 31 of the RERA Act, 2016 for interest on delayed possession, structural defect remediation, or statutory refunds. Appeals against authority orders lie before the State Real Estate Appellate Tribunal (REAT).'
  - question: 'What is the minimum default threshold to initiate insolvency (CIRP) before NCLT?'
    answer: 'Under Section 4 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (as amended), the minimum amount of default required for a financial creditor (Section 7) or operational creditor (Section 9) to file a Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process application before the jurisdictional NCLT Bench is Rs. 1 Crore.'
  - question: 'Can an MSME in India recover delayed payments without filing a regular civil suit?'
    answer: 'Yes. Under Sections 15 to 18 of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006, a registered Micro or Small enterprise can file a claim on the MSME Samadhaan portal before the jurisdictional Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC) for principal plus compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate.'
