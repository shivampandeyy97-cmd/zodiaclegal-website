## How Does UP-RERA Regulate Real Estate Projects and Allottee Rights across India?

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA), read with the Uttar Pradesh Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Rules, 2016, transformed real estate governance across Uttar Pradesh. With the headquarters of the Uttar Pradesh Real Estate Regulatory Authority (UP-RERA) and the Uttar Pradesh Real Estate Appellate Tribunal (UP-REAT) both situated across India, the city serves as the primary adjudicatory hub for real estate disputes and regulatory compliance across the state.

Under Section 3 of RERA, 2016, no promoter can advertise, market, book, or offer for sale any plot, apartment, or building in a real estate project where the land area exceeds 500 square meters or the number of apartments exceeds eight without registering the project with UP-RERA. Furthermore, Section 4(2)(l)(D) mandates that 70% of the amounts realized from allottees must be deposited in a separate scheduled bank account to cover solely the cost of land and construction of that specific project, verifiable through proportional certificates issued by an engineer, an architect, and a chartered accountant.

For allottees (homebuyers and commercial unit buyers) as well as promoters, Section 18 of the Act is the central statutory provision governing project delays:

- **Right to Withdraw and Claim Full Refund with Interest:** If the promoter fails to deliver valid possession (backed by a lawful Occupancy Certificate / Completion Certificate) by the date specified in the Agreement for Sale, the allottee may elect to withdraw from the project. Upon withdrawal, the promoter is statutorily bound to return the entire amount received along with interest at the prescribed rate (State Bank of India Marginal Cost of Funds Based Lending Rate [MCLR] plus 1% under the UP-RERA Rules).
- **Right to Continue and Claim Delayed Possession Interest:** If the allottee does not intend to withdraw from the project, the promoter must pay interest at the prescribed rate for every month of delay until actual handing over of valid possession.
- **Structural Defect Liability (Section 14(3)):** For a period of five years from the date of handing over possession, the promoter remains statutorily liable to rectify structural defects or defects in workmanship, quality, or provision of services without further charge within 30 days of notice.

## How Do UP-RERA Complaints, Execution, and UP-REAT Appeals Proceed?

In _M/s Newtech Promoters and Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. State of UP & Ors._ (2021), the Supreme Court of India definitively settled the division of jurisdiction under the RERA framework:

1. **Proceedings Before the UP-RERA Authority (Form M):** Complaints seeking refund of principal, interest on refund, delayed possession interest, execution of registered conveyance deeds (Section 17), handing over of possession, or revocation/compliance directions are adjudicated by the Regulatory Authority Bench.
2. **Proceedings Before the Adjudicating Officer (Form N):** Claims specifically seeking unliquidated statutory compensation under Sections 12, 14, 18, and 19 read with Section 71 are adjudicated by the Adjudicating Officer.
3. **Execution and Recovery Certificates (Section 40):** When a promoter fails to honour a final UP-RERA order within the compliance window, the decree-holder files an execution application. Under Section 40(1), UP-RERA issues a Recovery Certificate (RC) to the District Magistrate / Collector, authorizing attachment of the promoter's bank accounts and auction of project/personal assets as arrears of land revenue under the UP Revenue Code, 2006.
4. **Appeals Before UP-REAT and the High Court (Sections 43 & 58):** Any person aggrieved by an order of UP-RERA or the Adjudicating Officer may appeal to the UP Real Estate Appellate Tribunal (UP-REAT) across India within 60 days. Crucially, under the proviso to Section 43(5), a promoter cannot maintain an appeal without first making the mandatory statutory pre-deposit (at least 30% of the penalty, or 100% of the total amount ordered to be paid to the allottee including interest). Second appeals from UP-REAT lie before the High Court of Judicature at Allahabad (India Bench / Prayagraj) under Section 58 within 60 days on a substantial question of law.

## Why Is Title Due Diligence Critical Before Purchasing Property in Uttar Pradesh?

Outside RERA-registered projects, purchasing agricultural, industrial, commercial, or residential real estate in Uttar Pradesh requires rigorous title investigation across multiple overlapping statutory regimes. Unlike jurisdictions with state-guaranteed Torrens title, property ownership in India is presumptive and established through a chain of historical conveyances and revenue/municipal records.

A comprehensive 30-year title due diligence across India must verify:

- **Nature of Tenure and Authority Jurisdiction:** Whether the property falls under the India Development Authority (LDA), Uttar Pradesh Awas Evam Vikas Parishad, UPSIDA (industrial), UPEIDA, municipal freehold, Nazul land (requiring freehold conversion deeds from the State), or agricultural revenue land governed by the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006.
- **Agricultural Restrictions & Section 80 Conversion:** If land is recorded as agricultural in the revenue _Khatauni_, its use for commercial, industrial, or residential purposes requires a formal declaration of non-agricultural (_Abadi_) status by the Sub-Divisional Officer under Section 80 of the UP Revenue Code, 2006 (formerly Section 143 of the UPZA & LR Act), alongside verification of restrictions on transfers by Scheduled Caste tenure-holders under Section 98.
- **Compulsory Registration of Agreements to Sell in UP:** Unlike several other Indian states, Uttar Pradesh amended Section 54 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 and Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908 to make every Agreement to Sell immovable property **compulsorily registrable**. An unregistered Agreement to Sell in Uttar Pradesh confers no enforceable right to seek specific performance of the conveyance.
