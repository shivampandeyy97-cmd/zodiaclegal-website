## How Does Employment and Labour Law Govern Workplaces in Uttar Pradesh?

Unlike jurisdictions with a single unified "at-will" employment regime, workplace regulation in India—and specifically in Uttar Pradesh—operates across a dual statutory framework. An employee's legal rights and an employer's termination obligations depend first on the nature of the establishment and second on the actual functional duties performed by the individual employee.

For commercial offices, IT companies, hospitals, retail businesses, and service enterprises in Lucknow and across Uttar Pradesh, the primary state statute is the **Uttar Pradesh Dookan Aur Vanijya Adhishthan Adhiniyam, 1962** (the UP Shops and Commercial Establishments Act). This statute regulates establishment registration, daily and weekly working hour ceilings, overtime computation, national/festival holidays, earned and medical leave accumulation, and statutory notice requirements for discharge under Section 19.

Simultaneously, the **Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** and the **Uttar Pradesh Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** classify employees into two distinct legal categories:

1. **Workmen (Section 2(s) Central Act / Section 2(z) UP Act):** Any person employed to perform manual, unskilled, skilled, technical, operational, clerical, or lower-level supervisory work. Indian Labour Courts look at the **dominant nature of duties** rather than a designation like "Executive" or "Software Engineer." Terminating a workman who has completed 240 days of continuous service in a 12-month period (for reasons other than proven disciplinary misconduct) constitutes "retrenchment" and requires strict compliance with Section 25F of the Central Act / Section 6N of the UP Act (mandatory notice or wages in lieu thereof, retrenchment compensation of 15 days' average pay for every completed year of service, and statutory intimation).
2. **Non-Workmen (Managerial and Administrative Personnel):** Senior executives, department heads, and employees exercising genuine managerial, administrative, or policy-making authority are governed primarily by their written Employment Contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 alongside applicable provisions of the UP Shops and Commercial Establishments Act.

## How Should Employment Contracts and Restrictive Covenants Be Drafted?

Because Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 renders post-termination non-compete restraints void as an unlawful restraint of trade, copying foreign employment templates into Indian appointment letters creates a false sense of security. To protect proprietary business information, client relationships, and intellectual property without running afoul of Section 27, employment agreements in India must be structured around enforceable clauses:

- **In-Term Exclusivity & Moonlighting Prohibitions:** Restricting an employee from engaging in any concurrent employment, consultancy, or competing business *during* the subsistence of employment is fully valid and enforceable under Indian law.
- **Confidentiality & Trade Secret Protection:** Detailed definitions of confidential commercial, technical, and customer data, coupled with mandatory data-return and deletion undertakings upon exit.
- **Non-Solicitation of Clients and Employees:** Narrowly drafted covenants prohibiting a departing employee from actively soliciting specific clients served by them or poaching key team members for a reasonable post-termination window.
- **Paid Garden Leave Clauses:** Requiring a resigning senior employee to remain on the company's payroll during their contractual notice period (e.g., 60 or 90 days) while relieving them of active duties and revoking access to live client pipelines and proprietary repositories.
- **Work-for-Hire & IP Assignment (Section 17, Copyright Act, 1957):** Explicit clauses confirming that all inventions, source code, designs, and documentation created during employment vest in the employer, paired with a moral rights waiver and power of attorney to execute IP filings.

## What Are the Mandatory Requirements Under the POSH Act, 2013?

The **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013** (POSH Act) and the Rules framed thereunder impose strict, non-negotiable obligations on every employer across India:

- **Mandatory Constitution of Internal Committee (Section 4):** Every workplace employing **10 or more employees** (counting permanent, temporary, ad-hoc, interns, and contract workers) must constitute an Internal Committee (IC) by written order. The Presiding Officer must be a woman employed at a senior level, not less than half of the IC members must be women, and one member must be an external representative from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment.
- **Time-Bound Inquiry Procedure (Sections 9–13):** A complaint must be submitted within 3 months of the incident (extendable by 3 months). If conciliation is not requested by the aggrieved woman, the IC conducts an inquiry in accordance with the principles of natural justice, completing the inquiry within **90 days** and submitting its report to the employer within 10 days thereafter.
- **Annual Report & Board Report Disclosures:** The IC must submit an annual compliance report to the employer and the District Officer (Section 21), and every company governed by the Companies Act, 2013 must explicitly disclose compliance with the POSH Act in its Annual Board's Report under Rule 8(5)(x) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014.

## How Should Employers Handle Misconduct Inquiries and Separations?

When terminating an employee for cause—such as financial irregularity, data theft, habitual absenteeism, or insubordination—summary dismissal without following due process frequently results in Labour Court reinstatement orders with back wages or civil damages for wrongful termination. A defensible disciplinary process requires issuing a specific **Charge Sheet / Show-Cause Notice**, providing relied-upon documents, granting a reasonable opportunity to submit a written explanation, and conducting an impartial **Domestic Inquiry** recorded in writing before passing a reasoned termination order.
