## How Do GST Adjudication and Show-Cause Proceedings Operate in Uttar Pradesh?

For trading, manufacturing, construction, and service enterprises operating across India and across Uttar Pradesh, Goods and Services Tax (GST) compliance has shifted from routine return filing to automated data-matching scrutiny and high-stakes adjudication under the **Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017** and the **Uttar Pradesh Goods and Services Tax (UPGST) Act, 2017**.

Automated analytics on the GST portal routinely cross-verify a taxpayer's outward supplies (GSTR-1), auto-populated Input Tax Credit statements (GSTR-2B), summary tax returns (GSTR-3B), e-way bill generation logs, and Income Tax (Form 26AS / AIS) turnover disclosures. When discrepancies appear, tax administration typically proceeds through a structured statutory escalation:

1. **Return Scrutiny (Section 61 & Form GST ASMT-10):** The Proper Officer scrutinizes returns to verify correctness and issues a notice in Form GST ASMT-10 calling for an explanation within up to 30 days. Submitting a thorough reconciliation in **Form GST ASMT-11** backed by chartered accountant working papers and statutory explanations often leads to closure in Form GST ASMT-12 without an escalation to adjudication.
2. **Non-Fraud Adjudication (Section 73):** Where tax appears short-paid or Input Tax Credit (ITC) wrongly availed for reasons _other_ than fraud, wilful misstatement, or suppression of facts, the Proper Officer issues a Show Cause Notice in Form GST DRC-01 under Section 73. If the taxpayer pays the tax and applicable interest before the issuance of the notice—or within 30 days of the issuance of the Section 73 notice—no penalty is leviable under Section 73(5) and 73(8).
3. **Fraud / Suppression Adjudication (Section 74 & Section 74A):** Section 74 applies strictly where tax evasion is alleged by reason of **fraud, wilful misstatement, or suppression of facts**. Because a Section 74 order carries a mandatory 100% penalty, department officers sometimes invoke Section 74 mechanically for routine accounting or reconciliation errors. A well-structured legal reply challenges the foundational jurisdiction of a Section 74 notice if the notice fails to specify particularised acts of deliberate suppression or fraudulent intent. _(For financial years 2024–25 onwards, Section 74A governs demand notices with unified limitation timelines while retaining separate penalty slabs for fraud versus non-fraud defaults)._

## What Procedural Safeguards Protect Taxpayers Under Section 75 of the CGST/UPGST Act?

Even where a substantive tax or Input Tax Credit dispute exists, Section 75 of the CGST/UPGST Act, 2017 imposes strict mandatory discipline on adjudicating authorities. Non-compliance with Section 75 vitiates the resulting **Form GST DRC-07** demand order:

- **Mandatory Personal Hearing (Section 75(4)):** An opportunity of personal hearing **shall** be granted where a written request is received from the person chargeable with tax or penalty, _or where any adverse decision is contemplated against such person_. The High Court of Judicature at Allahabad (both at the India Bench and Prayagraj) has consistently held that passing an adverse order without affording an effective oral hearing—or fixing the date of personal hearing before the deadline for filing the written reply—violates Section 75(4) and the principles of natural justice.
- **No Travel Beyond the Show Cause Notice (Section 75(7)):** The amount of tax, interest, and penalty demanded in the final order shall not be in excess of the amount specified in the notice, and **no demand shall be confirmed on the grounds other than the grounds specified in the notice**.
- **Reasoned Speaking Order (Section 75(6)):** The Proper Officer must set out the relevant facts and the specific legal basis for his decision in the order. Cryptic orders that merely record that the taxpayer's reply was "found unsatisfactory" without dealing with the evidence submitted are vulnerable to appellate or writ interference.

## How Do Section 107 First Appeals and High Court Writ Petitions Work?

When an adverse adjudication order (Form GST DRC-07), a registration cancellation order (Form GST REG-19), or a transit penalty order under Section 129(3) (Form GST MOV-09) is passed, the primary statutory remedy is a **First Appeal under Section 107** of the CGST/UPGST Act before the Appellate Authority (the Additional Commissioner Grade-2 [Appeals], State Tax, or Commissioner [Appeals], CGST across India).

Under Section 107(1) and 107(4), the appeal in **Form GST APL-01** must be filed within **3 months** from the date of communication of the order, with the Appellate Authority empowered to condone a delay of at most **1 additional month** upon sufficient cause being shown. Because the Appellate Authority has no statutory power to condone delay beyond the aggregate 4-month (3 + 1 month) ceiling under Section 107, monitoring the GST portal's "Additional Notices and Orders" tab is critical for every registered enterprise.

Where an order is passed wholly without jurisdiction, in violation of Section 75(4) natural justice mandates, or where a bank account is attached arbitrarily under Section 83, taxpayers may invoke the extraordinary writ jurisdiction of the **High Court of Judicature at Allahabad, India Bench** under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.
