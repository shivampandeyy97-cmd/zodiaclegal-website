## What Legal Foundations Do Startups and Emerging Enterprises Need from Day One?

Early-stage ventures and growing Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) in Uttar Pradesh often focus exclusively on product development and sales until a co-founder dispute, an investor due diligence check, or a major client default exposes gaps in their legal structure. Establishing clean corporate hygiene early prevents expensive restructuring during capital raises.

Under Indian law, a startup intending to raise institutional or angel capital almost invariably incorporates as a Private Limited Company under the Companies Act, 2013 rather than a partnership or LLP, because equity investors subscribe to Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS), Compulsorily Convertible Debentures (CCDs), or equity shares with defined liquidation preferences and anti-dilution protections.

Before or immediately upon incorporation with the Central Registration Centre (CRC) and the Registrar of Companies (RoC), Kanpur, co-founders should execute a comprehensive Founders' Agreement and incorporate its core governance terms into the company's Articles of Association (AoA). This framework must establish:

- **Equity Split and Reverse Vesting:** Typically a 4-year vesting period with a 1-year cliff, ensuring that a co-founder who exits after a few months does not walk away with a large block of dead equity on the capitalization table.
- **Good Leaver vs. Bad Leaver Consequences:** Clear definitions of termination for cause (fraud, gross negligence, breach of non-compete, or abandonment of duties) versus voluntary resignation or disability, coupled with pre-agreed share transfer pricing mechanisms.
- **Intellectual Property Assignment:** Written assignment of all pre-incorporation and ongoing code, trademarks, designs, domains, and trade secrets from the individual founders and early contractors to the corporate entity under the Copyright Act, 1957 and Trade Marks Act, 1999.

## How Do DPIIT Startup India and Uttar Pradesh Startup Policies Operate?

Entities incorporated as a Private Limited Company, registered Partnership Firm, or LLP that are working toward innovation, development, or improvement of products or processes with a scalable business model can apply for recognition with the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) under Notification G.S.R. 127(E), provided they are within 10 years of incorporation and their annual turnover has not exceeded ₹100 crore in any financial year.

DPIIT recognition unlocks critical corporate law relaxations under the Companies Act, 2013:

1. **ESOP Flexibility (Rule 12, Share Capital and Debentures Rules):** Unlike standard private companies, DPIIT-recognized startups are permitted to grant Employee Stock Options (ESOPs) to promoters and directors holding more than 10% of the company's equity for up to 10 years from incorporation.
2. **Convertible Notes (Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014):** Recognized startups may raise funds via Convertible Notes—an instrument evidencing receipt of ₹25 lakh or more initially in a single tranche, convertible into equity or repayable within 10 years—without being treated as prohibited public deposits under Section 73.
3. **State-Level Incentives (StartInUP):** Startups registered in Uttar Pradesh with DPIIT recognition can additionally apply under the Uttar Pradesh Startup Policy (administered through the UP Electronics Corporation Limited / StartInUP portal across India) to evaluate eligibility for sustenance allowances, prototype grants, and patent filing reimbursements subject to prevailing state policy guidelines.

## How Are Seed Fundraising Rounds and ESOP Pools Executed?

When a startup raises an angel, pre-seed, or seed round, the transaction moves through four distinct legal phases:

- **Term Sheet Negotiation:** Defining pre-money valuation, investment amount, ESOP pool creation (whether pre-money or post-money), board seat/observer rights, reserved matters (affirmative voting items), founder lock-in, and exclusivity ("no-shop" period).
- **Legal & Financial Due Diligence:** Investor counsel audits the startup's MCA filings (Forms INC-20A, AOC-4, MGT-7, PAS-3), cap table accuracy, IP ownership agreements, employment contracts, tax filings, and pending disputes.
- **Definitive Agreements (SSA & SHA):** Drafting and negotiating the Share Subscription Agreement (containing conditions precedent, closing deliverables, and founder/company warranties) and the Shareholders' Agreement (governing ROFR, Tag-Along, Drag-Along, anti-dilution, liquidation preference, and information rights).
- **Closing & Post-Closing Filings:** Passing board and special resolutions (Form MGT-14), obtaining a valuation report from a Registered Valuer under Section 247, circulating private placement offer letters (Form PAS-4), receiving funds into a designated separate bank account, allotting securities within 60 days, and filing Form PAS-3 (and Form FC-GPR on the RBI FIRMS portal if foreign investors participate).

## How Does the MSMED Act, 2006 Protect Enterprises Against Delayed Payments?

For manufacturing and service MSMEs operating across India, Kanpur, and Uttar Pradesh, working capital lock-up due to delayed B2B payments is a frequent operational threat. Enterprises registered on the Udyam portal as **Micro** or **Small** Enterprises enjoy powerful statutory protection under Chapter V of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006:

- **45-Day Maximum Payment Ceiling (Section 15):** Even if a purchase order or contract specifies a 60-day or 90-day credit period, Section 15 overrides private contracts and mandates payment within 45 days from the day of acceptance or deemed acceptance of goods or services.
- **Mandatory Compound Interest (Section 16):** On failure to pay within the statutory timeline, the buyer is liable to pay compound interest with monthly rests to the supplier at **three times the bank rate** notified by the Reserve Bank of India, notwithstanding anything contained in any agreement between the parties.
- **Tax Disallowance (Section 43B(h), Income Tax Act, 1961):** Buyers who fail to pay Micro or Small Enterprises within the Section 15 timeline cannot claim the unpaid expense as a tax deduction in that financial year, creating strong commercial leverage during pre-litigation recovery notices.
- **MSEFC Conciliation & Arbitration (Section 18):** Unpaid suppliers can file a reference on the MSME Samadhaan portal before the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC) in Uttar Pradesh for time-bound statutory conciliation and arbitration.
