## How Does Commercial Litigation Operate Under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015?

When a commercial counterparty breaches a contract, withholds undisputed payments, or threatens to encash a bank guarantee wrongfully, choosing the right procedural forum and strategy determines both the speed of relief and the ultimate recovery. In India, business disputes are primarily resolved through designated Commercial Courts under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 or through domestic arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

For disputes falling within the jurisdiction of India and adjoining districts in Uttar Pradesh, the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 introduces a specialized, fast-track procedural regime that significantly modifies the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (CPC) for commercial disputes exceeding the specified pecuniary value of ₹3 lakh:

- **Mandatory Pre-Institution Mediation (Section 12A):** Unless a suit contemplates genuine urgent interim relief (such as an immediate injunction against asset dissipation or trademark infringement), the plaintiff must first initiate Pre-Institution Mediation before the District Legal Services Authority. A settlement reached in Section 12A mediation has the status and enforceability of an arbitral award under Section 30(4) of the Arbitration Act.
- **Strict 120-Day Forfeiture for Written Statements (Order VIII Rule 1 CPC as amended):** Unlike ordinary civil suits where procedural delays can stretch for years, a defendant in a commercial suit forfeits the right to file a Written Statement if it is not filed within an absolute maximum of 120 days from the date of service of summons.
- **Statement of Truth & Full Documentary Disclosure (Order XI CPC as amended):** Parties must file all documents in their power, possession, control, or custody along with the Plaint or Written Statement, supported by a mandatory sworn "Statement of Truth" under Appendix I.
- **Case Management Hearings & Summary Judgment (Order XIII-A CPC):** Commercial Courts hold Case Management Hearings (Order XV-A) to fix strict dates for evidence and oral arguments, and are empowered under Order XIII-A to pass Summary Judgment without recording oral evidence where the plaintiff or defendant has no real prospect of succeeding on the claim or defence.

## How Are Arbitral Proceedings Conducted Under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996?

Where the underlying agreement contains a valid arbitration clause under Section 7 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, civil courts are statutorily required under Section 8 to refer the parties to arbitration if an objection is raised no later than the date of submitting the first statement on the substance of the dispute.

The lifecycle of a domestic commercial arbitration seated across India typically involves:

1. **Notice Invoking Arbitration (Section 21):** Arbitration formally commences on the date the respondent receives a written notice under Section 21 explicitly invoking the arbitration clause, setting out the nature of the disputes, the quantum of claim, and proposing arbitrator names in accordance with the contract. Under Section 43, the Limitation Act, 1963 applies to arbitrations just as it does to court proceedings.
2. **Interim Measures by Court or Tribunal (Sections 9 & 17):** Before the arbitral tribunal is constituted, a party can approach the Commercial Court under Section 9 for urgent interim protection—such as securing the amount in dispute, preserving goods, or appointing a receiver. Once the tribunal is constituted, applications for interim relief ordinarily lie before the tribunal under Section 17, whose interim orders are directly enforceable as court orders.
3. **Appointment of Arbitrators (Section 11):** If the counterparty refuses to concur on an arbitrator within 30 days of the Section 21 notice, or if the contract contains an invalid unilateral appointment clause hit by Section 12(5) and the Seventh Schedule, the claimant files a petition under Section 11(6) before the High Court of Judicature at Allahabad, India Bench (for arbitrations seated within its territorial jurisdiction).
4. **Time-Bound Adjudication (Section 29A):** For domestic arbitrations, Section 29A mandates that the arbitral award shall be made within 12 months from the date of completion of pleadings (under Section 23(4)), extendable by up to 6 months by mutual consent of the parties, and thereafter only by an order of the Court for sufficient cause.

## How Are Arbitral Awards Challenged and Executed?

Once an arbitral tribunal renders a final award, the award is final and binding on the parties under Section 35. An aggrieved party has 3 months (extendable by a maximum of 30 further days upon showing sufficient cause, with no condonation permissible thereafter) to file an application to set aside the award under Section 34 of the Arbitration Act before the jurisdictional Commercial Court.

Under Section 36(2), merely filing a Section 34 challenge does **not** automatically stay the operation of an arbitral award. To obtain a stay of a money award under Section 36(3), the judgment debtor must typically deposit the awarded sum or furnish bank security as directed by the court. Once the award becomes enforceable, it is executed under Section 36(1) in the same manner as a decree of a civil court under Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure—including attachment and sale of movable and immovable properties and attachment of bank accounts.

## When Should Creditors Use Section 138 NI Act Alongside Civil Recovery?

When a commercial debtor issues a cheque toward discharge of a legally enforceable debt or liability and the cheque is dishonoured for insufficiency of funds or because the payment was stopped, the payee can initiate parallel criminal prosecution under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 alongside civil recovery or arbitration.

Under Section 143A of the NI Act, the trial court trying a Section 138 offence is empowered to direct the drawer of the cheque to pay interim compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount to the complainant within 60 days of framing the substance of accusation. However, because Section 138 timelines (30-day statutory notice, 15-day cure period, and 30-day complaint filing window under Section 142) are strictly construed, immediate document verification upon receiving the bank return memo is essential.
