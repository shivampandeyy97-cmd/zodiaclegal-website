Incorporating a company in Uttar Pradesh requires strict adherence to the statutory framework of the **Companies Act, 2013**, and the **Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014**. While initial incorporation applications are examined centrally by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) through the Central Registration Centre (CRC), every company with its registered office in Lucknow, Noida, Kanpur, or any other district in Uttar Pradesh falls under the regulatory supervision of the **Registrar of Companies (ROC), Kanpur**.

Promoters, institutional investors, and growing enterprises frequently encounter rejection or resubmission notices during incorporation due to defective object clauses, mismatched subscriber KYC records, or non-compliant registered office documentation. This guide sets out the exact procedural sequence, statutory filings, stamp duty norms in Uttar Pradesh, and mandatory post-incorporation milestones required to establish a legally compliant corporate entity.

---

## How Does the SPICe+ (INC-32) Incorporation Process Work in Uttar Pradesh?

The SPICe+ (Simplified Proforma for Incorporating Company Electronically Plus) Form INC-32 is an integrated web-based application on the MCA V3 portal consisting of Part A for name reservation and Part B for incorporation, DIN allotment, PAN/TAN issuance, EPFO/ESIC registration, and bank account opening in a single workflow.

Under Sections 4, 7, 12, and 152 of the Companies Act, 2013, promoters have two procedural routes when filing SPICe+:
1. **Two-Step Filing:** Submitting Part A first to reserve up to two proposed corporate names, awaiting CRC approval (valid for 20 days from approval), and subsequently completing Part B.
2. **Simultaneous Filing:** Submitting Part A and Part B together in a single integrated submission, where one proposed name is evaluated alongside the full incorporation docket.

### Step-by-Step Procedural Sequence

* **Step 1: Procurement of Class-3 Digital Signature Certificates (DSC):** All proposed subscribers to the Memorandum and proposed directors must obtain Class-3 DSCs with Aadhaar-based or video e-KYC verification, and register their DSCs on the MCA V3 portal under the relevant business user role.
* **Step 2: Name Availability Analysis under Rule 8:** Before filing Part A, the proposed corporate name must be checked against existing companies, Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs), and registered or pending trademarks across relevant classes under the Trade Marks Act, 1999. Names that are overly generic, identical, or phonetically similar to existing entities—or that imply patronage of the State or Central Government without prior approval under the Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950—face summary rejection.
* **Step 3: Drafting e-MoA (INC-33) and e-AoA (INC-34):** The Memorandum of Association defines the principal business objects in alignment with National Industrial Classification (NIC) 2008 codes. The Articles of Association govern internal management, board quorum, share transfer restrictions under Section 2(68), and affirmative voting rights if founders or early investors have agreed on specific governance terms.
* **Step 4: Filing Linked Forms (AGILE-PRO-S and INC-9):** SPICe+ must be accompanied by Form AGILE-PRO-S (INC-35) for mandatory EPFO and ESIC registration, professional tax (where applicable), bank account selection, and optional GSTIN application, alongside the electronic subscriber and director declaration in Form INC-9 verifying non-conviction and truthfulness of records.

| MCA V3 Form | Statutory Purpose | Governing Provision | Filing Stage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SPICe+ Part A** | Name reservation (up to 2 names) | Section 4(4) & Rule 8/9 | Pre-incorporation or simultaneous |
| **SPICe+ Part B (INC-32)** | Incorporation, DIN (up to 3 directors), PAN & TAN | Sections 7, 12 & 152 | Simultaneous or within 20 days of Part A |
| **e-MoA (INC-33)** | Electronic Memorandum of Association | Section 4 & Schedule I | Linked with SPICe+ Part B |
| **e-AoA (INC-34)** | Electronic Articles of Association | Section 5 & Schedule I | Linked with SPICe+ Part B |
| **AGILE-PRO-S (INC-35)** | GSTIN, EPFO, ESIC, and Bank Account | Rule 38A | Linked with SPICe+ Part B |
| **Form INC-9** | Auto-generated declaration by subscribers & directors | Section 7(1)(c) & Rule 15 | Linked with SPICe+ Part B |

## What Documents and KYC Records Are Required for Subscribers and the Registered Office?

To incorporate a company in Uttar Pradesh, promoters must provide self-attested identity and address proofs for all directors and subscribers, along with a valid registered office proof in Uttar Pradesh (utility bill not older than two months, rent agreement if leased, and owner's NOC) under Section 12.

Scrutiny officers at the Central Registration Centre routinely issue resubmission remarks when the name, date of birth, or residential address on a subscriber's supporting document does not exactly match their Income Tax PAN database record.

### Mandatory Documentation Checklist

1. **For Indian Nationals (Subscribers & Directors):**
   * Permanent Account Number (PAN) card (mandatory for all Indian nationals).
   * Identity Proof: Voter ID, Passport, Driving License, or Aadhaar Card.
   * Residential Address Proof (Distinct from Identity Proof): Bank Account Statement, Electricity Bill, Telephone Bill, or Mobile Bill in the applicant's exact name, dated within the preceding **60 days**.
   * Director Identification Number (DIN), if already held; otherwise, up to three DINs are allotted directly through SPICe+ Part B.
2. **For Foreign Nationals / Corporate Subscribers:**
   * Passport and foreign residential address proof, duly notarized and apostilled (for Hague Convention countries) or consularized by the Indian Embassy in the home jurisdiction.
   * Where an existing company or LLP subscribes to shares, a certified copy of the Board Resolution under Section 179(3) authorizing the subscription and designating an authorized representative to sign e-MoA and e-AoA.
3. **For the Registered Office in Lucknow or Uttar Pradesh:**
   * Utility bill (Electricity Bill, Water Bill, or Gas Bill) depicting the complete premises address and owner name, dated within the last **two months**.
   * Unconditional No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the premises owner permitting the proposed company to use the address as its registered office.
   * Executed Lease or Rent Agreement if the property is leased in the name of a promoter or the proposed company. Alternatively, if the exact address is not finalized at filing, promoters may specify a correspondence address and subsequently file **Form INC-22** within **30 days** of incorporation to verify the permanent registered office.

## How Are Stamp Duty and MCA Filing Fees Calculated in Uttar Pradesh?

Under the MCA zero-fee initiative, companies incorporated with an authorized share capital up to Rs. 15,00,000 pay zero base registration fee on Form INC-32, though promoters must still pay state-specific electronic stamp duty to the Government of Uttar Pradesh on the INC-32, MoA, and AoA forms, plus PAN and TAN fees.

Stamp duty on company incorporation documents is governed by the **Indian Stamp Act, 1899, as amended in its application to Uttar Pradesh**, and is collected electronically through the MCA portal at the time of SPICe+ Part B submission.

| Component | Applicability in Uttar Pradesh | Key Calculation Parameter |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SPICe+ (INC-32) Base Fee** | Zero for Authorized Capital up to Rs. 15,00,000 (Normal fee applies only above Rs. 15 Lakh) | Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014 |
| **UP Stamp Duty on MoA (INC-33)** | Fixed statutory duty rate prescribed by the State of Uttar Pradesh | Flat duty irrespective of authorized capital slab |
| **UP Stamp Duty on AoA (INC-34)** | Slab-based or percentage-based stamp duty in Uttar Pradesh | Scales with the Authorized Share Capital amount |
| **PAN & TAN Application Fees** | Mandatory statutory processing charges collected via MCA | Fixed NSDL/Protean processing fee for corporate PAN & TAN |

Promoters structuring early-stage entities in Lucknow or Noida typically keep initial authorized share capital between **Rs. 1,00,000 and Rs. 15,00,000** to optimize upfront incorporation expenses, increasing authorized capital later via **Form SH-7** (Ordinary Resolution under Section 61) when institutional equity rounds are raised.

## What Are the Mandatory Post-Incorporation Filings with ROC Kanpur?

Once the Certificate of Incorporation (Form INC-11) is issued, a newly incorporated company must hold its first Board Meeting within 30 days, appoint its first statutory auditor, open a corporate current account, collect subscription money, issue share certificates within 60 days, and file Form INC-20A within 180 days.

Receiving the Certificate of Incorporation (which embeds the Corporate Identity Number or CIN, PAN, and TAN) does not authorize the company to start commercial operations or exercise borrowing powers immediately. Section 10A of the Companies Act, 2013 imposes a strict post-incorporation condition precedent.

### Post-Incorporation Compliance Timeline

| Statutory Milestone | Governing Section / Form | Mandatory Deadline | Consequence of Default |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Board Meeting** | Section 173(1) & Secretarial Standard-1 (SS-1) | Within **30 days** of incorporation | Penalty on officers in default under Section 118/173 |
| **Verification of Registered Office** (if not filed in SPICe+) | Section 12(2) / **Form INC-22** | Within **30 days** of incorporation | Rs. 1,000/day penalty up to Rs. 1,00,000; strike-off risk |
| **First Statutory Auditor Appointment** | Section 139(6) / **Form ADT-1** (recommended) | By the Board within **30 days** (or by EGM within 90 days) | Board loses power; members must appoint at an EGM |
| **Allotment & Share Certificate Issuance** | Section 56(4)(a) / **Form SH-1** + UP Stamp Duty | Within **60 days** of incorporation | Penalty of Rs. 50,000 on company and officers |
| **Commencement of Business Declaration** | Section 10A(1)(a) / **Form INC-20A** | Within **180 days** of incorporation | Company penalty of Rs. 50,000; officer penalty up to Rs. 1,00,000; ROC Kanpur strike-off |
| **Significant Beneficial Owner (SBO) Filing** (if applicable) | Section 90 / **Form BEN-2** | Within **30 days** of receiving Form BEN-1 from SBO | Heavy monetary penalties and NCLT restrictions on shares |

Additionally, once physical share certificates (Form SH-1) are executed under the authority of the Board within two months of incorporation, the company must pay requisite **share issuance stamp duty** under the Indian Stamp Act within 30 days of issuance. Private companies that do not qualify as "Small Companies" under Section 2(85) must also evaluate dematerialization of securities under **Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules**.

## How Should Promoters Structure Governance in the Articles of Association (AoA)?

While standard Table F Articles of Association suffice for straightforward two-founder companies, ventures involving unequal equity contributions, sweat equity, or angel investors should adopt customized Articles at incorporation or immediately thereafter to govern share transfers, board seat rights, drag-along/tag-along provisions, and deadlock resolution.

Under the MCA V3 framework, the electronic AoA (Form INC-34) allows applicants to entrench specific clauses under **Section 5(3) of the Companies Act, 2013**. Entrenchment ensures that critical governance protections—such as founder veto rights, pre-emptive rights on new share issuances, or Right of First Refusal (ROFR) on secondary transfers—cannot be amended merely by a standard 75% Special Resolution without satisfying a higher agreed threshold (such as unanimous consent of specific shareholders).

Furthermore, companies establishing operations in Lucknow, Kanpur, or Noida must integrate state-level operational registrations alongside ROC Kanpur filings, including registration under the **Uttar Pradesh Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1962** (via the Nivesh Mitra single-window portal), Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration where turnover or interstate supply thresholds apply, and Udyam MSME registration if eligible.
