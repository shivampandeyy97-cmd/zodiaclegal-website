When a corporate borrower, infrastructure developer, or large commercial buyer defaults on substantial financial obligations, the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC)** provides the most potent statutory mechanism in Indian corporate law. Unlike a civil recovery suit, Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT) OA, or arbitration—where the debtor's existing promoters remain in control of the company throughout years of litigation—admission of a **Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)** petition immediately **suspends the defaulting company's Board of Directors under Section 17** and hands operational and financial control to an independent **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)**.

At the same time, the Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized (including in _Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India_ and _Transmission Corporation of Andhra Pradesh Ltd. v. Equipment Conductors and Cables Ltd._) that the IBC is a **beneficial resolution statute and not a mere debt-recovery forum** for disputed claims. For creditors and corporate debtors dealing with companies registered in Uttar Pradesh before the **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Allahabad Bench at Prayagraj**, understanding the fundamental distinction between **Financial Creditors (Section 7)** and **Operational Creditors (Sections 8 & 9)** is paramount.

## What Is the Difference Between a Financial Creditor (Section 7) and an Operational Creditor (Section 9)?

Under Section 5(7) and 5(8) of the IBC, a Financial Creditor is owed a "financial debt" disbursed against the consideration for the time value of money (such as bank loans, debentures, or corporate guarantees), whereas under Section 5(20) and 5(21), an Operational Creditor is owed an "operational debt" arising from the supply of goods, services, employment, or government dues.

The procedural rights, evidentiary burdens, and voting powers on the **Committee of Creditors (CoC)** differ sharply between the two categories:

| Legal Parameter                 | Financial Creditor (Section 7)                                                                                                                                   | Operational Creditor (Sections 8 & 9)                                                                                                                                     |
| :------------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Governing Definition**        | **Section 5(7) & 5(8):** Debt along with interest, if any, disbursed against the consideration for the **time value of money**.                                  | **Section 5(20) & 5(21):** Claim in respect of the **provision of goods or services**, including employment or statutory dues.                                            |
| **Mandatory Pre-Filing Notice** | **None required under statute** (Recall/Default notice under loan agreement suffices).                                                                           | **Mandatory 10-Day Demand Notice** in **Form 3** or **Form 4** under **Section 8(1)** is an absolute condition precedent.                                                 |
| **NCLT Application Form**       | **Form 1** (Rule 4 of Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016).                                                            | **Form 5** (Rule 6 of the Adjudicating Authority Rules, 2016).                                                                                                            |
| **Effect of a Dispute**         | A mere dispute over the exact quantum does **not** bar admission so long as undisputed default exceeds **Rs. 1 Crore** (_Innoventive Industries v. ICICI Bank_). | Any plausible **"Pre-Existing Dispute"** raised before the Section 8 notice mandates rejection of the petition (_Mobilox Innovations_).                                   |
| **Joint Filing Allowed?**       | **Yes**—Multiple Financial Creditors can file jointly to meet or exceed the Rs. 1 Crore threshold.                                                               | **No**—Each Operational Creditor must independently satisfy the Rs. 1 Crore default threshold (_Sadashiv Nomaya Nayak_).                                                  |
| **Voting Seat on CoC**          | **100% Voting Power** on the Committee of Creditors (excluding related parties under Section 21(2)).                                                             | **No Voting Right on CoC** (unless there are zero Financial Creditors), though entitled to attend CoC meetings if aggregate operational debt is $\ge 10\%$ of total debt. |

## How Does a Financial Creditor Initiate CIRP Under Section 7 Before NCLT Allahabad?

Under Section 7 of the IBC, 2016, a Financial Creditor (either by itself or jointly with other Financial Creditors) files an application in Form 1 before the NCLT Allahabad Bench furnishing record of the default from an Information Utility (NeSL) or other financial evidence, and proposing the name of a registered Resolution Professional to act as IRP.

Following the Supreme Court's foundational judgment in **_Innoventive Industries Ltd. v. ICICI Bank_ (2017)** and the clarification in **_Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd._ (2022)** (as subsequently reaffirmed within strict bounds in _M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank_, 2023), the NCLT's inquiry under Section 7 is focused on two core questions:

1. **Existence of a Financial Debt:** Verified through Sanction Letters, Facility Agreements, Debenture Trust Deeds, Corporate Guarantee Deeds, bank disbursement statements under the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891, and balance sheet acknowledgments of the Corporate Debtor (filed in Form AOC-4 with ROC Kanpur).
2. **Occurrence of a Default of Rs. 1 Crore or More:** Verified through **National E-Governance Services Limited (NeSL) Form D (Record of Default)** or CIBIL/CRILC records showing non-payment of whole or any part or installment of the debt when it became due and payable, within the 3-year limitation period under **Article 137 of the Limitation Act, 1963** (extended by any written acknowledgment of debt under Section 18 of the Limitation Act or part-payment under Section 19).

Crucially, for a Section 7 petition, naming a proposed **Interim Resolution Professional (IRP)** accompanied by their written consent in **Form 2** (confirming no pending disciplinary proceedings with IBBI) is **mandatory** under Section 7(3)(b).

## What Is the Step-by-Step Section 8 & Section 9 Process for Operational Creditors?

An Operational Creditor owed Rs. 1 Crore or more for unpaid goods or services must first deliver a statutory Demand Notice in Form 3 (or copy of invoice attached to Form 4) under Section 8(1) of the IBC. Only if the Corporate Debtor fails to pay or fails to notify a pre-existing dispute within 10 days can a Section 9 petition (Form 5) be filed.

Because Operational Creditors frequently face dismissal at the threshold due to technical defects in their Section 8 notice, strict compliance with the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016** is indispensable:

### Procedural Checklist for Section 8 & 9 Filings

1. **Service of Form 3 / Form 4 Demand Notice (Section 8(1) & Rule 5):**
   - Deliver the notice to the **Registered Office address of the Corporate Debtor** as appearing on the MCA Master Data (ROC Kanpur records) via Registered Post/Speed Post **and** electronically to the official email ID of a Whole-Time Director, Designated Partner, or Key Managerial Personnel shown on the MCA portal.
   - Attach Purchase Orders, Tax Invoices, E-Way Bills, Delivery Challans, GST GSTR-1/3B filings, and a tabular computation of dates of default (carefully excluding any invoice whose date of default falls within the prohibited **Section 10A COVID-19 suspension window between March 25, 2020 and March 24, 2021**).
2. **The 10-Day Statutory Window (Section 8(2)):** Within **10 days** of receipt of the Demand Notice, the Corporate Debtor must bring to the notice of the Operational Creditor either:
   - Record of full payment of the unpaid operational debt; or
   - Existence of a **dispute**, or record of the pendency of a suit or arbitration proceedings filed _before_ the receipt of such notice.
3. **Filing Form 5 Before NCLT Allahabad (Section 9):** If the 10-day period expires without payment or a valid pre-existing dispute reply, the Operational Creditor files **Form 5** supported by a mandatory affidavit under **Section 9(3)(b)** affirming that no notice of pre-existing dispute was received, along with bank statements/certificates and proof of NeSL filing.

## How Does the "Pre-Existing Dispute" Test Under _Mobilox Innovations_ Work?

In _Mobilox Innovations Pvt. Ltd. v. Kirusa Software Pvt. Ltd._ (2017), the Supreme Court held that if the Corporate Debtor demonstrates the existence of a plausible contention requiring further investigation that was raised prior to receipt of the Section 8 Demand Notice—and is not a patently feeble legal argument or spurious assertion—the NCLT must reject the Section 9 petition.

Whether advising an Operational Creditor prior to issuing a Form 3 notice or defending a Corporate Debtor before the **NCLT Allahabad Bench**, counsel must scrutinize the entire pre-notice email, WhatsApp, and letter trail:

- **For Operational Creditors:** Before serving a Section 8 notice, audit all prior communications to verify that the debtor never raised contemporaneous complaints regarding delayed delivery, defective quality, short supply, SLA penalties, or reconciliation disputes. If a genuine technical dispute exists, arbitration or a Commercial Court suit across India is the appropriate forum rather than NCLT.
- **For Corporate Debtors Defending Section 9:** Upon receiving a Section 8 Demand Notice, the debtor **must respond within the strict 10-day window under Section 8(2)**, annexing every prior email, quality rejection report, debit note, or arbitration invocation notice that predates the Section 8 notice. A "moonshine defense" fabricated for the first time _after_ receiving the Section 8 notice will be rejected by the NCLT, triggering admission of CIRP and suspension of the Board.
