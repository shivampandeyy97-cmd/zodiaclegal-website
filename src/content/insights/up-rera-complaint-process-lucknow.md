When a real estate developer fails to deliver possession of an apartment, plot, or commercial unit by the date stipulated in the Agreement for Sale, or deviates from sanctioned layout plans without obtaining two-thirds allottee consent under Section 14, the **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA Act)** read with the **Uttar Pradesh Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Rules, 2016** provides a dedicated, time-bound statutory forum.

For projects situated across central and eastern Uttar Pradesh—including Lucknow, Kanpur, Varanasi, Prayagraj, Agra, Ayodhya, and Gorakhpur—disputes are adjudicated by the **Uttar Pradesh Real Estate Regulatory Authority (UP-RERA) Headquarters Bench at Lucknow** (Kalakankar House, Old Hyderabad, Lucknow). Understanding the jurisdictional division between the Regulatory Authority and the Adjudicating Officer, the calculation of SBI MCLR+1% interest, and the post-decree Recovery Certificate (RC) mechanism is essential for securing effective relief.

## What Remedies Are Available to Allottees Under Section 18 of the RERA Act?

Under Section 18(1) of the RERA Act, 2016, if a promoter fails to give possession of an apartment, plot, or building in accordance with the terms of the Agreement for Sale by the specified date, the allottee has two distinct statutory options: either withdraw from the project and demand a full refund with interest, or continue in the project and receive interest for every month of delay until actual handover.

In ***Imperia Structures Ltd. v. Anil Patni* (2020)** and ***Newtech Promoters and Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. State of UP* (2021)**, the Supreme Court established that the allottee's right under Section 18(1) is an **unqualified statutory right** once the promised possession date (including any contractually valid force majeure grace period approved by the Authority) has expired:

| Statutory Election under Section 18 | Relief Granted by UP-RERA | Applicable Interest Period | Forum within UP-RERA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option A: Withdrawal & Refund** [Section 18(1)(a)] | 100% return of amount paid by the allottee + statutory interest (**SBI MCLR + 1%**) | From the respective dates of each installment payment until actual realization | **Regulatory Authority Bench** (Form M) |
| **Option B: Possession + Delay Interest** [Section 18(1)(a) Proviso] | Direction for physical handover with Occupancy Certificate (OC) / Completion Certificate (CC) + monthly delay interest (**SBI MCLR + 1%**) | From the promised date of possession in the Agreement for Sale until receipt of OC/CC and valid offer of possession | **Regulatory Authority Bench** (Form M) |
| **Option C: Structural Defect / Title Defect / Compensation** [Sections 12, 14(3), 18(2), 18(3)] | Rectification of structural defects within 5 years of possession, or monetary compensation | Determined based on actual loss, rent paid, and documentary proof | **Adjudicating Officer** (Form N) |

## How Does the Online Filing and Hearing Process Work Before UP-RERA in Lucknow?

A UP-RERA complaint is filed online through the official UP-RERA portal (`up-rera.in`) by registering as a litigant, selecting either the Conciliation Forum or Adjudication (Regulatory Authority / Adjudicating Officer), uploading the Agreement for Sale and payment ledger, and paying the prescribed Rs. 1,000 statutory filing fee.

UP-RERA follows an electronic e-Courts workflow designed to minimize procedural bottlenecks. However, incomplete pleadings or misclassified relief requests frequently lead to defective-filing objections or jurisdictional transfers.

### Step-by-Step Procedural Workflow Before UP-RERA Lucknow

1. **Pre-Filing Audit of the Agreement for Sale (BBA) & Payment Receipts:** Verify the RERA project registration number (`UPRERAPRJ...`), the exact date of possession promised in the Builder-Buyer Agreement (or Allotment Letter if the promoter failed to execute a registered BBA despite collecting more than 10% under Section 13), and compile a chronological Excel/tabular statement of every payment made via cheque, RTGS, NEFT, or home loan disbursement.
2. **Optional Conciliation Forum vs. Direct Complaint:** UP-RERA offers an RERA Conciliation Forum where a Conciliator mediates between the allottee and promoter with the assistance of consumer and CREDAI representatives. Where amicable resolution is unlikely or previous legal notices have gone unanswered, allottees proceed directly to file a formal complaint under **Section 31**.
3. **E-Filing Complaint on the UP-RERA Portal:**
   * Fill in the complainant, respondent (Promoter), and project particulars.
   * State the precise **Statement of Facts**, **Grounds of Violation** (citing Sections 11, 13, 14, or 18 as applicable), and **Specific Relief Sought** (clearly choosing between Refund with Interest vs. Possession with Delay Interest).
   * Upload indexed PDF annexures (Allotment Letter, BBA, Payment Receipts, Bank Loan Sanction/Tripartite Agreement, Demand Letters, and Prior Correspondence) and remit the Rs. 1,000 fee online.
4. **Notice, Reply by Promoter, and Virtual/Hybrid Bench Hearings:** Once registered, an automated notice is served on the promoter's registered dashboard and email requiring a written reply (Written Statement / WS). Hearings before the UP-RERA Lucknow Benches are conducted through structured e-Court cause lists, followed by written arguments and a reasoned final order under Section 31/38.

## What Defenses Do Promoters Raise in UP-RERA and How Are They Evaluated?

Promoters contesting delay complaints before UP-RERA typically plead *force majeure* (such as COVID-19 lockdowns or NGT construction bans), alleged default in installment payments by the allottee, or the issuance of a partial/deemed Completion Certificate.

When evaluating promoter defenses—whether representing an allottee seeking enforcement or advising a real estate developer on project compliance—the Lucknow Bench applies strict evidentiary standards:
* **Force Majeure Extensions:** UP-RERA recognizes only officially notified *force majeure* periods (such as the statutory six-month COVID-19 extension granted under Section 6 of the RERA Act in 2020). General market slowdowns, contractor disputes, or delays in obtaining routine municipal clearances from the Lucknow Development Authority (LDA) or Housing Board do not excuse the promoter from Section 18 liability.
* **Invalid "Offer of Possession" Without OC/CC:** Under Section 11(4)(b) and Section 19(10) of the RERA Act, a promoter cannot legally issue a binding Offer of Possession or levy holding/maintenance charges **without first obtaining the Occupancy Certificate (OC) or Completion Certificate (CC)** from the competent development authority. Any paper "Offer of Possession for Fit-Out" issued without an OC/CC does not stop the running of delay interest.
* **Construction-Linked Plan (CLP) Defaults:** If a promoter alleges that the allottee defaulted on milestone demands, the promoter must produce architect and engineer certificates proving that the corresponding physical construction milestone was actually achieved on site when the demand letter was raised.

## How Is a UP-RERA Order Executed When the Builder Fails to Pay or Hand Over Possession?

If a promoter fails to comply with a UP-RERA final order within the stipulated 45-day compliance window, the decree-holder allottee can file an online Execution Application on the UP-RERA portal under Section 40 of the RERA Act and Rule 23/24 of the UP-RERA Rules.

Securing a favourable order is only the first phase of recovery; disciplined follow-through at the execution stage is critical:

1. **Issuance of Recovery Certificate (RC) under Section 40(1):** For monetary orders (refund with interest or quantified delay interest), UP-RERA calculates the exact principal and accrued MCLR+1% interest and transmits an electronic **Recovery Certificate (RC)** to the **District Magistrate (Collector)** of the district where the project or promoter's assets are located (e.g., District Magistrate, Lucknow). The Collector and Tehsildar enforce the RC as **arrears of land revenue** under the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006—attaching project bank accounts, unsold inventory, and corporate assets.
2. **Enforcement of Non-Monetary Orders under Section 40(2) & Section 63:** Where the order directs physical handover of possession, execution of a registered Conveyance Deed (Sale Deed), or rectification of defects, UP-RERA exercises powers akin to a Civil Court under Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and may impose a continuing penalty under **Section 63** extending up to **5% of the estimated cost of the real estate project** for persistent non-compliance.
